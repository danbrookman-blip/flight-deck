01 · The Customer
Airship ICP: Business Profile

The ideal Airship customer is a UK hospitality operator that wants to prove marketing ROI through guest data, not just send emails. They have an integrated tech stack, a marketing resource with strategic thinking, and a genuine commitment to understanding visit frequency. Airship makes that possible.

Business Characteristics

  • UK-based multi-site hospitality operator: restaurant groups, pubs, bars, cafes, casual dining, family brewers
  • 2–200 locations; sweet spot 5–50 sites with dedicated marketing resource
  • Food-led venues with meaningful dwell time, guests visit to stay and sit down
  • Existing email or CRM activity, likely on a generic tool (Mailchimp, manual uploads) or fragmented across platforms
  • Ambition to prove marketing drives footfall, not just measure open rates
  • Either has a Rewards programme or a Loyalty partner, or is willing to build one

Performance Indicators

  • £20k+ weekly turnover per location (£1M+ annual revenue per site)
  • 4%+ of CRM base engaging with basket-level redemption, the benchmark for platform ROI
  • Active EPOS, booking, Wi-Fi, or feedback integration, at least 3 connected data sources
  • Marketing team runs campaigns with strategic intent, not "set and forget"
  • Visit frequency is a managed business metric, operators setting goals around 1.2 → 1.6 visits/year
  • Willing to invest in strategy, setup, and platform adoption

Technical Profile

  • EPOS integration active, basket data is essential for proving ROI through Proof of Presence
  • At least 3 integrations total: booking, Wi-Fi, feedback, PMS, or loyalty
  • Own branded website with ability to place signup forms (owns the guest value exchange)
  • CRM data managed by the operator, not reliant on manual uploads from third parties
  • Open to automated journeys, segmentation, and multi-channel communication (email, SMS)

Organisational Signals

  • Head of Marketing, Marketing Director, or Marketing Executive is the primary user and champion
  • Budget holder (MD, Finance Director, or Founder) understands marketing as a revenue driver
  • Not expecting the platform to "just work" without strategy, willing to invest in setup
  • Guest experience is a genuine business priority, ROI-focused, not just activity-focused
  • Open to working with Airship's team on journeys, campaigns, and automation strategy
01b · Boundaries
Who We Serve, and Who We Don't

Being clear on who Airship is not for is as important as defining who it is for. These are the boundaries that keep us focused on operators where we can deliver real, provable value.

✓ Strong Fit

  • Multi-site UK restaurant groups, pubs, cafes, family brewers
  • EPOS + 3 or more integrations connected (or clear path)
  • Dedicated marketing resource with strategic thinking
  • Food-led venues, guests sit down and dwell
  • Has or is building a Rewards or Loyalty programme
  • £1M+ annual revenue per location
  • 2–200 locations (sweet spot 5–50)
  • Wants to prove ROI through visit frequency data

✗ Not a Fit

  • Hotels, stay-based vs. visit-based model not yet aligned
  • Competitive socialising venues
  • Super-enterprise (350+ locations, different product needs)
  • Food-to-go or delivery-only, no in-venue dwell or visit frequency loop
  • Late night / nightclubs
  • Single-site with no growth plan or under 2 locations
  • No EPOS, or manual-only data approach
  • No dedicated marketing resource and no budget for one
  • Outside UK hospitality

02 · Buyer Personas
Who We're Selling To

Airship has three core buyer personas. Marketing drives adoption and day-to-day use. The budget holder controls the decision. Operations and Sales benefit from the platform but rarely lead the purchase. Each needs a different angle.

Primary User & Champion
Head of Marketing / Marketing Director

The daily user and internal champion. Wants to run sophisticated, data-driven campaigns without being at the mercy of agency timelines or manual data exports. Measures success in visit frequency uplift and campaign attribution.

Frustrated by: no way to prove campaigns drive footfall, fragmented guest data, generic tools that don't understand hospitality.

Cares About
  • Proof that marketing drives actual visits (PoP)
  • Automated journeys: birthdays, lapsed guests, welcome sequences
  • Segmentation based on real basket and visit data
  • Speed and self-service, no agency dependency
  • Airship AI for smarter analysis and content
Economic Buyer · Budget Decision
MD / Finance Director / Founder

Controls the budget. Needs to see a clear commercial case, cost per visit, revenue per campaign, frequency uplift, before committing. Airship needs to move from "marketing cost" to "revenue lever" in this conversation.

Frustrated by: marketing spend with no measurable return, tools that require expensive agency support to get value from.

Cares About
  • ROI, measurable revenue per campaign
  • Predictable flat-rate cost (not per-email billing)
  • Visit frequency as a board-level metric
  • Reducing dependence on third-party platforms
  • Competitive edge, what does Airship give us that others can't?
Secondary User · Sales & Events
Sales Manager / Events Manager

Uses Airship to identify opportunities, fill quiet periods, and manage private dining or event enquiries. Benefits from guest intelligence without necessarily owning the platform.

Cares About
  • Guest profiles for surprise & delight or upsell
  • Targeted campaigns to fill predictably quiet nights
  • Events and experiences marketing to the right segments
  • Enquiry tracking and pipeline visibility
Secondary User · Operations
Operations Manager / Customer Success

Relies on Airship for front-of-house insight, guest service recovery, and understanding visit patterns. Not the buyer, but a strong advocate when the platform is embedded in ops.

Cares About
  • Guest history at a glance for service recovery
  • Loyalty and rewards reconciliation
  • Understanding repeat vs. lapsed guest patterns
  • Reliable, automated guest communications
03 · Discovery
Questions to Confirm ICP Fit

These questions confirm whether the opportunity is the right fit for Airship and begin building the commercial case. The goal is to uncover the gap between what they're doing today and what Airship can prove for them.

  1. How many sites do you operate, and what does your growth plan look like over the next two years?
    Establishes size and trajectory. A 10-site group with a plan to double is a stronger prospect than one static at 30.
  2. What technology are you currently using: EPOS, bookings, Wi-Fi, feedback, order & pay? How are they connected?
    Maps the integration landscape. We need at least EPOS + 2 others for meaningful data consolidation and PoP. Fewer than 3 = amber flag.
  3. How are you currently doing email or CRM marketing, and how do you know if it's working?
    The "how do you know if it's working" question almost always surfaces the PoP gap. Most operators admit they can't prove it. That's the opening.
  4. Do you have a Rewards or Loyalty programme in place? How engaged is your CRM base with it?
    Basket-level engagement of 4%+ is the performance threshold. No Rewards and no Loyalty partner is a red flag, ask if they're open to building one.
  5. Who owns the guest database, and do you manage your own signup and opt-in process?
    Operators who don't own their website or manage their own signup forms can't own the value exchange. This is a structural blocker, flag early.
  6. What does success look like for your marketing in the next 12 months? Are you measuring visit frequency?
    Visit frequency (moving from 1.2 to 1.6 annual visits) is Airship's north star metric. Operators who aren't tracking it yet often become converts once they see PoP.
  7. Who in the business owns this decision, and who would need to sign off on a platform investment?
    Maps the buying process. Marketing champions it; Finance or the MD approves it. Get to the budget holder early or the deal will stall.
