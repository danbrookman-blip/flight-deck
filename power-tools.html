Part 2 · the craft and the power tools

You have the foundations. Now the craft.

Part 1 gets everyone productive: profile set, Brain found, on-brand by default. Part 2 is how the work gets genuinely good. Better context in, better output out, and four power tools that turn a one-off result into something repeatable, reusable and shippable.

THE CRAFT

Better context in, better output out.

After setup, the single biggest lever is the quality of what you give Claude. The same request with a clear goal, a real example and a stated standard returns a far better first draft. Anyone picks this up in a week.

Say who and what it is for

Name the audience and the job before the ask. "A renewal email to a wary venue owner" beats "write an email". Context up front saves three rounds of editing.

Show one example of good

Paste a past output you were happy with and say "match this". One concrete example teaches more than a paragraph of description.

Treat it as a draft, then refine

Expect the first pass to be 80%. Say what is off and push again. Two quick rounds beat one perfect prompt every time.

POWER TOOL 1

Styles. Your voice, on a switch.

A Style is a saved preset for how Claude writes - tone, format and length. Your profile sets who you are everywhere; a Style lets you flip the register for a particular job, then flip back.

Styles
90 sec · walkthrough coming soon
Owner: add the Loom share ID for "styles" in the video list at the foot of this page.
Watch, then do: how a Style works. Then draft your first one with the prompt below.

When to reach for it

A kind of writing that recurs and should sound consistent: a tight internal-update style, a customer-facing style, a Toggle stats-led style. Save it once, apply it with a click.

Style vs profile

Your profile is always on - who you are and the brand basics. A Style is chosen per task to shape one output. They stack: the profile sets the floor, the Style sets the register.

Draft a Style
Draft a Claude Style for [internal updates / customer comms / Toggle marketing]. Always: [tone], [format], [length]. Never: em dashes, emoji, vague claims. Lead with the outcome and prove it with a number. Give it a short name I can pick from the Style menu.
POWER TOOL 2

Skills. A repeatable job, packaged once.

A Skill packages a whole way of doing a task - the steps, the format, the standard - so it runs the same expert way every time, for anyone. Where a good prompt helps once, a Skill helps for good.

Turn a great output into a Skill
We do [task] often. Here is the best version we have produced: [paste]. Write a Skill spec for it - the steps, the format, the standard and what to avoid - so anyone gets this quality first time.
POWER TOOL 3

Artifacts. The output you keep, not just read.

An Artifact is a finished thing Claude builds in its own panel - a document, a one-pager, a working web page, a small tool - that you refine in place and keep. Different from a chat answer, which you read and lose.

POWER TOOL 4

Cowork. Delegate the whole job, not one step.

Cowork is the agentic step up. You hand over a multi-step job and Claude works across your connected tools to deliver it, with far less back-and-forth. Built for everyone, not just developers.

QUICK REFERENCE

Which tool, when.

Six places to put your effort, each for a different job. Most days you use the first two without thinking; reach for the rest as the work calls for it.

Profile

Always on. Who you are and the brand basics, carried into every chat. Set once. (Step 1.)

Style

A recurring kind of writing you want in a consistent voice. Switch it on per task.

Brain

Work that needs your department's shared knowledge, kept correct for everyone. (Step 2.)

Skill

A task done often and the same way. Package it once; anyone gets the expert version.

Artifact

The output is a deliverable to keep and refine, not just an answer to read.

Cowork

A multi-step job across several tools you want to delegate end to end.

The payoff

Automated Scorecard reporting. Keep the boards moving, the rest writes itself.

The EOS Scorecard used to be hand-collated every week. Now two live reports build themselves from the tools the team already uses. Move your deals in Pipedrive and your cards in Trello, and the numbers are ready for the L10. No exports, no chasing, no slide-building.

The whole idea

All you need to do is keep the boards moving.

The reporting is automatic. The only manual input is the work itself, tracked where it already happens. Healthy boards mean a Scorecard that is true, current and ready to walk, every single week.
