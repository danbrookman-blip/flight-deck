STEP 1

Set up you. The highest-leverage ten minutes you will spend.

Your profile is permanent memory that travels into every chat you ever have. Set it once and Claude knows your name, your role, how you work, and the brand rules, without you ever briefing it again.

I'm [name], [role] at Airship. I work across teams and need clarity and decisiveness.

I run two brands. When the work is clearly one brand, apply its rules. When it's unclear which, ask before designing or writing branded copy.

- Hyphens, never em dashes. No emoji in brand or product output.

- Zonal: fact only (acquired October 2022). Never a competitive frame or basis for advice.

Airship - the platform brand. Marketing surface is loud and gradient-forward; the in-app product is calm.
- Brand type Montserrat; product UI type Lato.
- Brand colours: purple #8d00d4, magenta #ec00ff, deep purple text #34004d. Purple gradient, never blue, used sparingly.
- Product colours: blue #3180FF, slate text #354B64, layered near-whites (#FFFFFF / #FAFBFB / #F4F6F7).

Toggle - Hospitality Commerce (usetoggle.com): presold revenue and CRM engagement via digital gift cards, experiences and tickets. Not "a gift card provider".
- Type Manrope (ExtraBold headlines and big numbers), Lato fallback.
- Colours: Toggle Blue #1770FD dominates (~60% blue / 30% white and neutral / 10% accent), cyan #45C1FF accent, Slate Ink #354B64 text. Backgrounds only white or Toggle Blue.

Flight Deck - the internal mark for Airship and Toggle work. Internal use only, never client-facing.
- Use it for anything that stays inside the company: team docs, decks, notes, internal tools, this hub.
- When a comm is going to a customer, use Airship or Toggle instead, by the brand the work belongs to.
- Pick by audience first: internal-only defaults to Flight Deck; client-facing uses Airship or Toggle.

Why this matters. Without a profile, every colleague re-briefs Claude on the basics in every chat, and outputs drift off-brand. With it set, the brand rules are on by default and you spend your time on the actual work.
STEP 2

The Brains. Your knowledge bases, and the first place to look.

A Brain is a Claude Project that holds everything Claude needs to know about one area of the business. There is a defined set of them, created and owned centrally, not a free-for-all. When you need something for your work, the right Brain is where you look first and the source of truth. Each carries a name so it is easy to talk about.

Find and use your Brain

A fixed set, not a free-for-all

A curated set covering knowledge, sales, marketing, design, engineering and how we run the company. You use and improve them; nobody spins up their own. Knowledge stays in one trusted place instead of scattered across hundreds of projects.

One owner each

Every Brain has a single owner who keeps its knowledge correct and current. Ownership is stewardship of a shared asset, not personal territory.

The first place to look

Before asking around or starting from scratch, open the right Brain. If the answer is not there, that is a gap to fill in the Brain, not a reason to work around it.

When a Brain is wrong, amend the Brain. If Claude returns something off or out of context, the knowledge in the Brain is wrong, missing or stale. Fix it there, so it is right for everyone the next time, rather than just patching it in the chat and moving on. That correction habit is the whole difference between a knowledge base people trust and a pile of one-off chats.

The improvement loop

Do not just use a Brain. Keep it right. Feed it what works, and fix what is wrong, and the next output starts from a better place. This is the owner's core habit.

MOVE 1

Build

Get an output to where you want it, refining in the chat as normal.

→
MOVE 2

Ask Claude to write what made it good, or what went wrong, as a reusable fix.

MOVE 3

MOVE 4

Compound

Next time the Brain is right first try. Repeat, and it sharpens every week.

Capture what worked
That output is exactly right. Write an updated instruction block I can paste into this project's instructions so future outputs come out like this first time. Keep it tight and specific: format, tone, length, and what to avoid.
Correct it when it is wrong

Build on what the Brain already knows
Have we worked on [topic] in this project before? If so, build on it rather than starting fresh, and tell me what you're carrying over.

STEP 5

Make it stick. The plays that get us to 100%.

Adoption is a behaviour, not a launch. These plays sit on top of the rollout plan and turn "we have Claude" into "this is how we work".

Make it stick
90 sec · walkthrough coming soon
Owner: add the Loom share ID for "step5" in the video list at the foot of this page.
Watch, then do: the 90-second close. Then pick one play and run it this week.

The adoption plays

1

One Brain per person on day one

Do not boil the ocean. Everyone joins the Brains that cover their work, starting with the knowledge bases Adelaide and Troy. That is enough to be productive.

2

Owners own their Brain

One named owner per Brain, accountable for its knowledge being right and for the outputs it produces.

3

Lead from the top at the L10

The Thursday L10 is the first real test. Leadership using Gino live is the strongest signal we can send.

4

Two quick wins per team, made visible

Each champion brings two documented wins to the senior L10. Visible value pulls the rest along.

5

Keep the Brains correct

When Claude gets something wrong, the owner amends the Brain, not just the chat. A source of truth is only useful while it stays true.

6

Feed and fix, weekly

Every owner adds one improvement and corrects one error in their Brain each week. That is how the loop becomes a habit.

Standing guardrails

  • No pricing or commercial commitments without human review.
  • Customer comms get a human sense-check before sending.
  • No customer personal data pasted in without checking the data policy.
  • Anything going externally needs human sign-off.
  • L10 actions confirmed by the meeting owner before distributing.
  • Owners review and refresh their Brain monthly.
