How the development cycle works

The AI does the planning and the typing. We do the deciding. Every change runs an AI-led loop with a person steering at the points that matter.

The three phases

Work out what to build, build it, then run it

Each phase is an AI-led loop with you steering, and each one hands richer context to the next. It is not a rigid waterfall - work flows back and forth as understanding improves.

Inception
What to build, and why

Turns a high-level Intent into a set of Units a Pod can build. The AI asks, drafts stories and risks, the Pod refines and groups them.

Comes outValidated Units: stories, NFRs, risks, a success measure, suggested Bolts.
Construction
How to build it

The build, in Bolts. Domain design, then logical design, then code and tests generated together - the Pod deciding the trade-offs at each gate.

Comes outTested, deployable code, traceable back to the story that produced it.
Thin for v1 Operations
Run it and keep it healthy

Deploy, observe, maintain. This is the least developed phase, and it is where our two live platforms spend most of their time.

For nowKeep production on our existing release and monitoring practice.

Brownfield first: because most work is changes to Airship and Toggle rather than new builds, the AI usually reads the existing code into context before proposing anything - the reverse-engineering step.

The work hierarchy

Intent breaks into Units, Units ship in Bolts

One vocabulary for how work is sized, from the goal down to the slice you build today.

01 · Intent
The goal

A high-level statement of what you want and why. The starting point the AI decomposes into real work.

02 · Unit
The block

A self-contained block one Pod can build without constant cross-talk to others. Roughly an epic.

One Pod owns it end to end
03 · Bolt
The slice

The smallest delivery cycle: plan to validated and done. What Scrum called a sprint, rebuilt to run short.

Hours to days, not weeks

A Unit is delivered through one or more Bolts, run in parallel or back to back.

Who runs it

A Pod is the team. A Mob is that team in session

Same people, two words for two states. No new ceremony to schedule - picture your Pod round a screen with the AI.

The standing team

Pod

A small, cross-functional team of 4-6 that owns an outcome end to end - not a layer, not a function.

  • A Pod lead clarifies, prioritises and keeps the Pods talking.
  • Mostly developers, ranging wider as the AI handles the specialist legwork.
  • No separate QA Pod - validating the AI's output is the Pod's own job.
The team in session

Mob

The Pod, plus whoever owns the Intent, working a phase with the AI on a shared screen.

  • Mob Elaboration - working an Intent into Units.
  • Mob Construction - building a Unit into code.
  • Mob Testing - validating what got built.

At our scale, realistically two Pods, maybe three if we lean on part-time specialists.

The guardrail that comes first

Human-in-the-Loop

The single sentence that defines the method: the AI plans and executes; humans decide and validate. The AI is never the final authority.

AIDoes the workProposes the plan, drafts the design and code, runs the tests.
■The gateA person reviews and signs off at every decision that matters.
AIAsks, never assumesOpen questions come to you as multiple choice with an "Other" option.
■Nothing on shaky groundWork is checked for contradictions and gaps before it advances.

Watch for process atrophy

The failure mode is drifting into rubber-stamping the AI instead of reviewing it - letting the agent decide everything. The gates exist so automation amplifies your judgment instead of quietly replacing it.

The signal to slow down

If you ever notice the Pod approving rather than reviewing, that is the cue to slow down at the checkpoints, not speed up.

Adaptive depth

Scale the process to the change, not the other way round

The release valve that keeps the method light for a small team. The AI proposes a path sized to the work; you check it and adjust. Two things scale, not one.

Dial 1

Breadth - which stages run

A defect fix skips requirements analysis and domain modelling and goes almost straight to the fix. A new feature gets the full run. The AI proposes which stages earn their place.

Dial 2

Depth - how far each goes

Even when a stage runs, it can run light or deep. A trivial utility needs no architecture deep-dive; a new billing flow does. The gates that keep you in control are never skipped.

"Not scripted, but steered." When in doubt, ask what this change actually needs - then do that much, and no more.
