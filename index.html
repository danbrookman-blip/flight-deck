START HERE

There is no single right way to use Claude.

Use a quick chat for a one-off question or a fast draft. Use your department Brain when the work needs the department's shared knowledge, has to stay on-brand, or should get better over time for the whole team. Neither is wrong. This plan makes the second kind reliable, without getting in the way of the first.

What Claude is to us

A capable assistant that takes a rough input and returns a clean, on-brand, structured output. It augments the tools we already use. It does not replace Fin in Intercom, Pipedrive, or Cursor.

Where your knowledge lives

Each department has one Brain: its knowledge base and the first place to look. There is a defined set, created and kept correct centrally, not hundreds of projects everyone spins up.

Who this is for

Everyone, on day one. Set up your profile, find your department Brain, and you are productive. The deeper craft comes in Part 2.

STEP 1

Set up you. The highest-leverage ten minutes you will spend.

Your profile is permanent memory that travels into every chat you ever have. Set it once and Claude knows your name, your role, how you work, and the brand rules, without you ever briefing it again.

1. Log in

Open Claude on web, desktop or the mobile app and sign in with your Airship account.

2. Open Settings, then Profile

This is your personal preferences block. It is invisible to colleagues and applies to every one of your chats.

3. Paste the block below

Add your name and role at the top, then paste the shared brand rules underneath. Save.

Paste into Settings > Profile
I'm [name], [role] at Airship. I work across teams and need clarity and decisiveness.

I run two brands. When the work is clearly one brand, apply its rules. When it's unclear which, ask before designing or writing branded copy.

Shared rules (both brands):
- UK spelling throughout (colour, organise, fulfilment, centre).
- Hyphens, never em dashes. No emoji in brand or product output.
- Lead with the outcome, prove it with a number. No vague claims, no flowery prose, no heavy corporate jargon.
- Zonal: fact only (acquired October 2022). Never a competitive frame or basis for advice.

Airship - the platform brand. Marketing surface is loud and gradient-forward; the in-app product is calm.
- Brand type Montserrat; product UI type Lato.
- Brand colours: purple #8d00d4, magenta #ec00ff, deep purple text #34004d. Purple gradient, never blue, used sparingly.
- Product colours: blue #3180FF, slate text #354B64, layered near-whites (#FFFFFF / #FAFBFB / #F4F6F7).

Toggle - Hospitality Commerce (usetoggle.com): presold revenue and CRM engagement via digital gift cards, experiences and tickets. Not "a gift card provider".
- Type Manrope (ExtraBold headlines and big numbers), Lato fallback.
- Colours: Toggle Blue #1770FD dominates (~60% blue / 30% white and neutral / 10% accent), cyan #45C1FF accent, Slate Ink #354B64 text. Backgrounds only white or Toggle Blue.
- Signature: the wave motif, rounded everything, stats as the hero, two-tone headlines, mobile-first. Wordmark lowercase "toggle", never a capital T.
Why this matters. Without a profile, every colleague re-briefs Claude on the basics in every chat, and outputs drift off-brand. With it set, the brand rules are on by default and you spend your time on the actual work.
STEP 2

The department Brains. Your knowledge bases, and the first place to look.

A Brain is a Claude Project that holds everything a department needs Claude to know. There is a defined set of them, created and owned centrally, not a free-for-all. When you need something for your area, the Brain is where you look first and the source of truth. Each carries a name so it is easy to talk about.

A fixed set, not a free-for-all

One Brain per department, plus a few shared head-office Brains. You use and improve them; nobody spins up their own. Knowledge stays in one trusted place instead of scattered across hundreds of projects.

One owner each

Every Brain has a single owner who keeps its knowledge correct and current. Ownership is stewardship of a shared asset, not personal territory.

The first place to look

Before asking around or starting from scratch, open your department Brain. If the answer is not there, that is a gap to fill in the Brain, not a reason to work around it.

When a Brain is wrong, amend the Brain. If Claude returns something off or out of context, the knowledge in the Brain is wrong, missing or stale. Fix it there, so it is right for everyone the next time, rather than just patching it in the chat and moving on. That correction habit is the whole difference between a knowledge base people trust and a pile of one-off chats.

How to use a Brain. Open it and say what you want plainly. It already holds the context, so skip the long briefing. The prompt below is for the one person who owns a Brain and is setting it up, not for everyone.
For a Brain owner · setting up the department Brain
You are helping me set up the Claude Project for the [team] department, called [Brain name].
Ask me the five brief questions one at a time - purpose, audience, outputs, standard, guardrails - then write a tight project-instruction block I can pin at the top of the project. UK spelling, hyphens not em dashes, no emoji, lead with the outcome.

STEP 4

Memory and improving. Keep the Brain right, and it compounds.

Claude remembers in layers. Knowing which layer holds what is the difference between starting from scratch every time and a Brain that nails your house style first try, and stays accurate because you keep it that way.

1
GLOBAL · EVERY CHAT

Your profile

Who you are and the brand rules. Set once in Settings, applies everywhere. (Step 1.)

2
PER BRAIN · PERMANENT

A Brain's knowledge and instructions

The files you add and the brief pinned at the top of the project. The Brain's fixed expertise.

3
PER BRAIN · BUILDS OVER TIME

A Brain's memory

What Claude carries forward from your past chats inside that project. It grows week on week. You can view, edit or clear it.

4
TEMPORARY

The chat

Your working space for one task. It ends when the chat ends. Fine for one-offs; for shared work, capture what matters into the Brain.

The improvement loop

Do not just use a Brain. Keep it right. Feed it what works, and fix what is wrong, and the next output starts from a better place. This is the owner's core habit.

MOVE 1

Build

Get an output to where you want it, refining in the chat as normal.

→
MOVE 2

Capture or correct

Ask Claude to write what made it good, or what went wrong, as a reusable fix.

→
MOVE 3

Save

Put it into the Brain's instructions or knowledge so it sticks for everyone.

→
MOVE 4

Compound

Next time the Brain is right first try. Repeat, and it sharpens every week.

Capture what worked
That output is exactly right. Write an updated instruction block I can paste into this project's instructions so future outputs come out like this first time. Keep it tight and specific: format, tone, length, and what to avoid.
Correct it when it is wrong
That was wrong: [what it got wrong and why]. Update this project's knowledge or instructions so it does not happen again, and tell me exactly what you changed.
Build on what the Brain already knows
Have we worked on [topic] in this project before? If so, build on it rather than starting fresh, and tell me what you're carrying over.
On-brand on demand, in any chat
Apply [Airship brand / Airship product / Toggle] styling and our UK-English, hyphens-not-em-dashes, no-emoji rules. Lead with the outcome and prove it with a number.
STEP 5

Make it stick. The plays that get us to 100%.

Adoption is a behaviour, not a launch. These plays sit on top of the rollout plan and turn "we have Claude" into "this is how we work".

The adoption plays

1

One Brain per person on day one

Do not boil the ocean. Everyone joins their department Brain and Athena. That is enough to be productive.

2

Owners own their Brain

One named owner per Brain, accountable for its knowledge being right and for the outputs it produces.

3

Lead from the top at the L10

The Thursday L10 is the first real test. Leadership using Porto live is the strongest signal we can send.

4

Two quick wins per team, made visible

Each champion brings two documented wins to the senior L10. Visible value pulls the rest along.

5

Keep the Brains correct

When Claude gets something wrong, the owner amends the Brain, not just the chat. A source of truth is only useful while it stays true.

6

Feed and fix, weekly

Every owner adds one improvement and corrects one error in their Brain each week. That is how the loop becomes a habit.

Standing guardrails

  • No pricing or commercial commitments without human review.
  • Customer comms get a human sense-check before sending.
  • No customer personal data pasted in without checking the data policy.
  • Anything going externally needs human sign-off.
  • L10 actions confirmed by the meeting owner before distributing.
  • Owners review and refresh their Brain monthly.

Pin this brief at the top of every Brain

Purpose

What problem does this Brain solve?

Audience

Who uses it, in what context?

Outputs

Standard

What does good look like?

Guardrails

What must it never do?

How we know it is working. Three numbers, reviewed at the champion sync: the share of each team with a profile set, the number of Brains being fed and kept correct each week, and quick wins logged. Adoption you cannot measure is adoption you cannot defend.
